# Demystify Verbosity Compensation Behavior of Large Language Models

## Provenance and scope read

Zhang, Das and Zhang report an empirical benchmark from Penn State University's Department of Computer Science and Engineering. The ACL Anthology records the paper in the Proceedings of the 2nd Workshop on Uncertainty-Aware NLP, in Suzhou, China, in November 2025. The proceedings PDF is dated November 9. Its title page and back matter identify the authors' academic affiliation and include an ethics statement. The reviewed paper has no separate funding or competing-interest disclosure. The author preprint, arXiv:2411.07858v2, has a longer title and some different headline numbers. Both report the same research project. The claim ledger uses the published workshop version. ## Reader's orientation

The paper asks what happens when a question calls for a compact answer and a model supplies extra material anyway. Across the tested model–dataset combinations, outputs include repeated wording, multiple proposed answers, imprecise substitutes, surplus explanation and presentation markup. The authors call the behavior *verbosity compensation* (VC). In most of their model–dataset comparisons, flagged answers have poorer gold-answer token recall. Longer answers also tend to score as more uncertain in the four models used for the uncertainty analysis. These findings directly inform **OB-12 Excess verbosity** for bounded answer formats. They give no estimate of unprompted default verbosity, developer review burden, or whether maintained knowledge files become bloated, stale or contradictory over time. [§§3.1–3.3, pp. 162–163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=3) · [§§5.1–5.3, pp. 164–167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=5)

## Detailed analytical reading

### The experiment sets a narrow expectation for the answer

Each input combines a source, a question and a prompt emphasizing a phrase-length response without recap or explanation. A known short reference answer makes it possible to inspect whether the additional text serves the requested answer. The authors describe sampling 600 instances per dataset and aiming for challenging questions with clear, short reference answers. Four constructed QA settings ask for facts from documents. Qasper uses questions about research papers. Their LongBench set merges HotpotQA, MuSiQue and 2WikiMultihopQA. NarrativeQA asks about book or movie material. NaturalQuestions_30 places the relevant information in the middle of a 30-document set. The fifth setting modifies MMLU by giving the original options as hints while asking for a generated answer. Table 7 makes the brevity requirement explicit throughout the input. Since the main study applies that instruction throughout, it establishes persistence under explicit requests for brevity. An unprompted baseline is outside the design. [§4.1, p. 163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=4) · [App. A.1, pp. 170–171](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=11) · [Table 7, p. 172](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=13)

The 14 systems come from the GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Gemma and Mistral families. The authors report model defaults for generation, local Hugging Face inference on eight A100s for the open models, and official APIs for GPT, Claude and Gemini. Table 8 identifies GPT-4o as `gpt-4o-2024-05-13` and Mistral-7B as the Instruct v0.3 checkpoint. Input preparation retains the source's leading sentences until the budget for a particular model, after allowing for the question and prompt, is filled. As a result, models with different context windows can receive different portions of the same document. The tested outputs depend on these historical versions, the available evidence in the input and the question design. [§4.2, p. 163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=4) · [Apps. A.2–A.3 and Alg. 2, p. 171](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=12) · [Table 8, p. 173](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=14)

### The main detector labels redundancy relative to a reference answer

In the main study, the fraction of responses labeled verbose is the VC rate. The authors give a GPT-3.5-Turbo judge the definitions, demonstrations, question, prediction and gold answer and ask for a binary label. They report 93% agreement against their own manual labels on 100 samples. That agreement concerns the authors' annotation and judge in this setup. Agreement with other reviewers or performance on other genres is unmeasured. This definition also matters when comparing sections. The main detector is reference-based and gives a content-sensitive judgment. Figure 4 bins by output length, while the supplemental chain-of-thought and trip-planning experiments use step-length rules. Those results describe different operational measures. [§3.1, p. 162](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=3) · [§4.2, p. 163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=4) · [§§5.2–5.3 and Fig. 4, pp. 166–167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=7) · [App. C.2, p. 174](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=15)

All tested model–dataset combinations show some flagged responses. For example, GPT-4o has a 50.40% flagged rate on NarrativeQA, 31.79% on Qasper and 15.00% on modified MMLU. The same source's table lists 29.39% as GPT-4o's average across the five datasets. The variation within a model is relevant to assistants used for different types of work. The paper's separate qualitative analysis examines six selected model–dataset combinations and restricts annotations to answers already flagged as verbose and with recall below 1. The five labels cover ambiguity, question repetition, enumeration, excess detail and formatting. A model may respond with an imprecise description in place of a number or propose several candidates when the task seeks one. The shares change within this restricted subset. For Gemini 1.5 Flash, repetition accounts for 67.86% in the annotated MMLU subset and enumeration for 47.62% in the annotated Qasper subset. Those percentages describe forms within the annotated subset. They do not estimate the fraction of all answers containing those forms. [§5.1 and Fig. 2, p. 164](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=5) · [Fig. 3 and Table 1, p. 165](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=6) · [App. B.1, Table 9, p. 173](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=14)

### The quality gap measures recall of reference tokens

For each answer the authors measure the share of gold-answer tokens that appear in it. Their main contrast, Δ, is mean recall among judged-concise answers minus mean recall among judged-verbose answers for that model and dataset. Their relative contrast divides Δ by the model's overall recall on that dataset. Recall gives a verbose response credit for including gold tokens even alongside extraneous or conflicting material. A list containing one correct option and several incorrect options can have perfect recall. That choice makes it harder to explain a positive gap as an automatic scoring penalty for extra tokens, while leaving factual precision and usefulness only partly captured. [§3.2, pp. 162–163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=3)

The average rows in Tables 2–3 give Δ values of 13.48 percentage points on Qasper, 6.17 on LongBench, 4.36 on NarrativeQA, 7.69 on NQ30 and 10.90 on the modified MMLU set. Several cells run the other way. In NarrativeQA, Claude 3.5 Sonnet has recall of 50.77% for concise outputs and 56.29% for verbose outputs. On Qasper, Llama-3-70B has a 24.12-point gap according to Table 2 (52.86% versus 28.74%). The introduction reports a Qasper example of 24.72%, and §5.2 gives 24.7% for Llama-3-70B on Qasper. The between-group comparison reflects which questions landed in each group. Appendix C.4 examines model predictions on common test instances and still reports positive group gaps, including 16.22 points on Qasper. This supplementary comparison spans models and leaves open a same-model, same-question causal effect of editing away verbosity. [§1, p. 161](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=2) · [§5.2, p. 164](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=5) · [Table 2, p. 165](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=6) · [Table 3, p. 166](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=7) · [App. C.4, Table 13, pp. 175–176](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=17)

The study compares the relative gap against Arena Elo and log context capacity across the 14 models. For the three QA sets organized by source length, the reported context-window correlations are −0.26 (Qasper), −0.53 (LongBench) and −0.61 (NarrativeQA). The MMLU and Natural Questions rows are near zero for the reported capacity correlations. Table 6 prints the latter row as NQ14, although the surrounding text and task inventory refer to NQ30. These comparisons describe the evaluated checkpoints. Dataset, model and how much of a document fits can vary together. The evidence leaves open whether a single model would change in the same way if supplied with more context. [§5.2, p. 165](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=6) · [Table 6, p. 167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=8) · [App. B.3 and Fig. 7, pp. 172, 176](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=17)

### Uncertainty tracks length at the group level

The authors use LM-Polygraph tools and group outputs by length in tokens. For Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8×7B and Llama-3-8B they use perplexity. For GPT-3.5-Turbo the methods describe summing eigenvalues of a graph Laplacian. Figure 4 averages over the five datasets, displaying a general rise in the uncertainty scores as outputs lengthen, with some local fluctuations. The scales differ across methods. The plot supports a within-model association for these short-answer settings. It does not give a calibrated likelihood that a particular answer is uncertain from length alone, a threshold suitable for other tasks, or an experimental test of cause. [§3.3, p. 163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=4) · [§5.3 and Fig. 4, pp. 166–167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=8)

The authors propose an explanation through first-token behavior. In selected Qasper examples, some concise answers begin with a reference-answer token whose probability is concentrated, while some flagged answers begin with a generic opener, punctuation or framing. An opener can require more tokens to reach a complete answer and can delay a checkable result. The evidence consists of selected softmax distributions. The study does not intervene on first tokens or determine how often that account explains the association. [Fig. 1, p. 161](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=2) · [§5.3, p. 166](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=7) · [App. B.2, Table 10, pp. 172, 175](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=16)

### The supplementary tests probe different definitions of excess

The chain-of-thought experiment asks for short visible reasoning steps on sampled questions from Qasper and modified MMLU. The text says 100 samples from two datasets without specifying the allocation. It flags a response when any step crosses the length rule, irrespective of the number of steps. The prose asks for fewer than 10 tokens, whereas the printed formula flags lengths greater than 10. The exact boundary at 10 is therefore ambiguous. Table 4 reports a 24.54-point gap for GPT-3.5-Turbo on modified MMLU (80.95% recall for concise answers and 56.41% for verbose ones). Appendix C.2 applies a similar rule with a prompt phrased in words to 500 sampled trip-planning problems. It reports 51.49% and 12.87% flagged for Llama-3-8B and Llama-3-70B respectively. These are observations under explicit step-length constraints. They cannot establish that explanations of that length are inherently redundant. [§5.2, p. 166](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=7) · [Table 4, p. 167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=8) · [App. C.2, Table 11, pp. 174–175](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=16) · [App. C.7, Table 16, pp. 177–178](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=19)

In Appendix C.3, all three concision prompts compared on Llama-3-8B still produce positive recall gaps on Qasper and modified MMLU. The shortest instruction yields the highest overall recall among those tested variants on both sets (63.63% and 61.07% respectively). All three include a form of concision cue. The variant comparison leaves open whether adding one improves recall, or whether rewriting an already generated long answer makes it correct. [App. C.3 and Table 12, pp. 174–176](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=17)

### Regenerating a flagged answer can change the final output in the benchmark

The basic cascade starts with the weaker model and requests a fresh generation from the next model when the detector flags the current response. Algorithm 1 uses the reference answer as part of its judgment and returns the final model's response if none passes before the list ends. As reported in the Qasper column of Table 5 and §5.4, Mistral-7B alone has 63.81% VC, GPT-4o alone has 31.79%, and the cascade has 16.60%. Table 14 confirms the Qasper cascade rate. The abstract and introduction give 16.16% for Qasper. That number appears in the average column of Table 5. The same table prints an apparent Mistral average of 74.19%, above every displayed dataset value in its row. Table 9 repeats 74.19% and differs from Tables 5 and 14 on Mistral's NarrativeQA rate. This dossier uses the supported Qasper cells and leaves the conflicting Mistral aggregate unresolved. [§3.4 and Alg. 1, p. 163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=4) · [§5.4, p. 166](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=7) · [Table 5, p. 167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=8) · [Table 9, p. 173](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=14) · [Table 14, p. 177](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=18)

The distinct routing experiment assumes an unknown gold answer at inference time. It calibrates a length cutoff to the mean gold-answer token length from 100 training samples and assigns different probabilities of escalating outputs above and below the cutoff. The authors compare average token costs and answer F1 against randomized routing, and for selected combinations against perplexity-based routing. The resulting cost–F1 evidence is about tested pairs and short-answer formats with reference data for calibration. It differs from setting a general-purpose documentation length threshold or asking one model to abbreviate text it already produced. [App. A.4 and Fig. 6, pp. 171–174](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=12) · [App. C.1 and Fig. 8, pp. 174, 177](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=18)

Appendix B.4 (printed p. 172) tests a specific shortening intervention on already verbose Llama-3-8B responses to Qasper. Keeping the first four tokens reduced reported recall from 44.93% to 43.13%, a loss of 1.80 percentage points. This mechanical truncation differs from editing a response for meaning. The appendix baseline differs from Table 2’s 53.51% verbose recall, so the two comparisons should be kept separate. [Appendix B.4](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf)

## Extractable practitioner insights

### Look for a missing answer inside a long response

For a bounded software task, first establish which content is required to verify the answer. If a developer asks for the current name and default of a configuration field, a checkable reply could include the field, its default, and the declaration path. A reply that paraphrases the request and lists plausible field names leaves the identification work unfinished. The study's taxonomy and recall comparison give a reason to inspect that pattern, especially if the output substitutes generic background for the requested fact. A developer could restate the unresolved point, inspect the declaration, and allow an explicit unknown when the available evidence is insufficient. This is a practitioner adaptation for OB-12. Neither accuracy gains from that workflow nor the handling of coding tools was measured by the paper. **SRC-0017:C02, C04–C06, C08.** [§§4.2, 5.1–5.3, pp. 163–167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=4)

### Match the answer format to the decision

A short, reference-backed format is feasible when the user needs a status, identifier, default value or other bounded result. For a diagnosis, design review or migration note, the necessary content can include rationale, tradeoffs and tests. The experiments place constraints on excess relative to a declared answer shape. The authors' qualification on useful context supports preserving evidence or rationale that the downstream decision actually needs. Asking which clause helps the reader decide or verify is a practitioner adaptation. No universal word count follows from the experiments. **SRC-0017:C01–C02, C10, C13.** [Table 7, p. 172](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=13) · [Ethics Statement and Limitations, p. 168](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=9)

## Related work, conflicts, and limitations

- **OB-12 is partially supported.** The paper demonstrates avoidable material under a compact-answer expectation. It gives no direct data on human verification time, signal-to-noise in software documentation, or document rot. Such outcomes would require a study of document use or repeated updates, with criteria for relevance, redundancy, staleness and contradiction. [§4, p. 163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=4) · [Limitations, p. 168](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=9)
- **Input position and output verbosity are different outcomes.** Zhang et al. reuse the middle-position split from the Natural Questions experiment in SRC-0001. That shared data source limits independence for claims concerning that configuration. Their output-length and verbosity findings add measurements on this split and other datasets. Earlier instructions losing influence across a conversation fall outside this design. [SRC-0001 dossier](SRC-0001--lost-in-the-middle.md) · [App. A.1, pp. 170–171](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=11)
- **Task and measure shape the association.** The judge sees the gold answer, the reference is assumed concise, and the key quality metric credits reference-token overlap. The uncertainty analysis groups by length. It therefore cannot distinguish all effects of question difficulty, answer length and content structure. A bare answer can be wrong, and an answer rich in relevant evidence can be long. [§§3.1–3.3, pp. 162–163](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=3) · [§5.3 and Fig. 4, pp. 166–167](https://aclanthology.org/2025.uncertainlp-main.14.pdf#page=8)
- **The PDF gives conflicting headline and table values.** Qasper cascade frequency is 16.60% in both result tables that report it for Qasper. Table 2 gives a 24.12-point Qasper recall gap for Llama-3-70B. The status of the conflicting headline numbers and the Mistral average remains unresolved.
