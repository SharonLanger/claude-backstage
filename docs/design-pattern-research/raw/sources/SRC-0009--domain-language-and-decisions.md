## Reader's orientation

The workflow addresses two kinds of information that can disappear after a design discussion. A shared term can lose its agreed meaning, leaving the user, reviewer and coding agent to make different assumptions. An architectural choice can lose its rationale, leaving later readers to guess why an apparently simpler implementation was rejected. Pocock assigns the first kind of information to a concise glossary and the second to an architectural decision record (ADR) with a higher threshold for creation. [What it does](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#what-it-does) · [Two artifacts, two bars](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#two-artifacts-two-bars)

The article offers an inspectable process for exposing disagreement and preserving agreed facts. Its FAQ raises the possibility that a plain-English description produces the same agent behavior as a domain term. The article supplies neither a comparison of the two nor a test of whether an ADR prevents unwanted edits. Treat team alignment and recoverable rationale as the uses described. Gains in model reliability, prompt compression and decision retention remain unmeasured. [Common questions, glossary value](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#common-questions)

## Detailed analytical reading (original words)

### Resolve the ambiguity while it still affects a decision

Pocock’s examples expose different problems a design discussion can catch. Two speakers saying *cancellation* may disagree about which operation the term describes. A label like *Account* can also hide different entities. The article uses Customer and User as an example of a split. Before persisting either kind of decision, use a concrete case whose outcome differs depending on the interpretation. This is particularly useful when the person and the agent agree on the nouns while assuming different limits on the behavior. [What it does](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#what-it-does) · [When to reach for it, scenario table](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#when-to-reach-for-it) · [Skill definition, During the session](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/domain-modeling/SKILL.md#during-the-session)

In the article’s code example, the user talks about cancellation at the level of part of an order, while the implementation cancels whole orders. The skill is supposed to bring the conflict back to the user before editing. From a workflow perspective, the disagreement leaves a decision open. The existing behavior may be intentional, the user may be describing new behavior, or the description and code may reflect different domain boundaries. Ask what should happen in the specific case before treating either account as authoritative. The source documents checking the code, glossary and ADRs. The issue tracker is outside that default scope. [Cross-referencing, and where it stops](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#cross-referencing-and-where-it-stops)

### Keep the glossary useful at the point of use

Pocock calls for an inline update when a term has been agreed. Record its meaning in one or two sentences and the displaced synonyms in `_Avoid_`. That makes the decision available as soon as the conversation starts relying on it. The article favors using the agreed word where names become artifacts other people use, including module and table names, status enums, ticket titles and commands. Ordinary prose can still use natural variation. When a project already has a glossary and the task needs only a definition, reading the file suffices. [What it does](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#what-it-does) · [When to reach for it, scenario table](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#when-to-reach-for-it) · [Two artifacts, two bars](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#two-artifacts-two-bars) · [Common questions, glossary value](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#common-questions)

The tool creates a root `GLOSSARY.md` after the first resolved term when the repo has one domain context. A root `GLOSSARY-MAP.md`, where present, points to the glossaries for different contexts. The format file says to determine the relevant context, asking when the answer is unclear. Pocock says to remove implementation detail and accumulated decisions from an overgrown glossary before considering a split. A split is justified when a lean document still spans domains that readers need to approach separately. The guide favors deliberate scaffolding for a mature repo without a glossary, since waiting for individual sessions to build it can be slow. One user reportedly answered more than 50 questions before their glossary was ready. The count describes that report only. [Prerequisites](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#prerequisites) · [Common questions, glossary size and initial scaffolding](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#common-questions) · [Glossary format, Single vs multi-context repos](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/domain-modeling/GLOSSARY-FORMAT.md#single-vs-multi-context-repos)

### Record where the obvious edit would undo a trade-off

Pocock’s skill offers to record a choice only when three criteria all apply. Reversing it has a meaningful cost, a future reader is likely to wonder why it was made, and the team actually considered alternatives with different consequences. The discussion should give the team enough context to accept or decline the offered record. The default record is numbered under `docs/adr/` and can be one to three sentences giving the circumstances, choice and reasoning. The companion format permits more detail where it earns its place, such as a superseded status, noteworthy alternatives, or consequences likely to surprise a future reader. [Two artifacts, two bars](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#two-artifacts-two-bars) · [Skill definition, Offer ADRs sparingly](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/domain-modeling/SKILL.md#offer-adrs-sparingly) · [ADR format, Template and Optional sections](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/domain-modeling/ADR-FORMAT.md#template)

The format file illustrates the threshold with expensive-to-change database, messaging, authentication and deployment choices. Its examples also include a hypothetical compliance constraint that rules out AWS, a boundary that assigns ownership of customer data to one context, and a deliberate choice to use manual SQL when a later reader might expect an ORM. These explain why a choice could deserve a record. They are illustrations of decision types, with no observed outcomes for any provider or implementation. The skill definition shows that an established repository can also have system-wide and context-specific ADR locations. [ADR format, What qualifies](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/domain-modeling/ADR-FORMAT.md#what-qualifies) · [Skill definition, File structure](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/domain-modeling/SKILL.md#file-structure)

### Inspect the result and account for missing history

Pocock reports that a workflow which invokes `grilling` may skip `domain-modeling`, even when the surrounding instructions call for both. He suggests naming the skill directly if expected glossary work never appears. His indicators of intended behavior include a clarification interrupt, a live glossary edit after agreement, a cited code contradiction, and an explanation that a proposed ADR fails one of the three tests. Use these indicators to inspect a session where a decision was actually resolved or a contradiction actually existed. The source gives no measured invocation rate. [When to reach for it, invocation paragraph](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#when-to-reach-for-it) · [It's working if](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#its-working-if) · [Where it fits](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#where-it-fits)

If old issue threads hold naming decisions, the workflow can rediscover a settled problem. The linked reporter describes exactly that in a Django repo and says they added repository instructions to look for tracker history. Pocock’s article points to `docs/agents/domain.md` as the place to add that instruction. The FAQ also warns that an unreviewed glossary can make a speculative definition look settled to subsequent sessions. Taken together, these cases motivate checking what was actually resolved and where its supporting evidence lives before accepting a glossary change. [Cross-referencing, and where it stops](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#cross-referencing-and-where-it-stops) · [Issue #717, Where I hit this and Workaround we used](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/717) · [Common questions, glossary value and vague prompts](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#common-questions)

## Extractable practitioner insights

- **BP-07 Semantic anchors.** Use a short, reviewed definition to make a recurring project term dependable for the people reading prompts, tickets and code. Disambiguate the term with a case, then use the settled term consistently at the naming boundary. This directly supports vocabulary discipline. The broader hypothesis that familiar words reliably activate learned model behavior, or yield equivalent output with fewer tokens, remains untested here. `SRC-0009:C01`, `C02`, `C07`. [What it does](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#what-it-does) · [Common questions](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#common-questions)
- **BP-09 Decision guards.** Give a future editor ready access to the rationale for a consequential, counterintuitive choice. The article supports a narrowly scoped ADR as a marker. Asking the editor to locate the relevant ADR before changing affected code, or linking to it from a nearby comment or test, would be catalog adaptations. The source provides no enforced workflow or data on unwanted edits. `SRC-0009:C04`, `C05`, `C09`. [Two artifacts, two bars](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#two-artifacts-two-bars) · [Cross-referencing, and where it stops](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#cross-referencing-and-where-it-stops)
- **AP-09 Silent misalignment and BP-02 Active partnership.** The whole-order example illustrates a useful prompt for pushback. When the agent finds a mismatch between the proposed behavior and the implementation, ask it to identify both readings and get explicit confirmation of the intended one before editing. This targets one concrete form of silent misalignment. The guide supplies a design example and no general measure of contradictory-instruction detection. `SRC-0009:C01`, `C03`, `C05`. [Cross-referencing, and where it stops](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#cross-referencing-and-where-it-stops)

### Worked adaptation for warehouse inventory

This example was created for the dossier. A warehouse team asks the agent to release stock at midnight, and existing tickets use *release* for both moving a pallet back into available inventory and authorizing a shipment to depart. Ask what should happen for a pallet already picked for a shipment but still physically present. The owner confirms that midnight expiration applies to holds on unpicked units. The glossary can define **Reservation Expiry** as the end of a hold on unpicked units and **Dispatch Authorization** as permission for a picked shipment to leave. Use those names when drafting the ticket and reviewing code references. Before implementing, ask the agent to point to the current behavior for the picked case and resolve any discrepancy with the owner. This applies the vocabulary and contradiction checks in `SRC-0009:C01–C03`. [What it does](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#what-it-does) · [Cross-referencing, and where it stops](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#cross-referencing-and-where-it-stops)

The team also chooses to keep allocation decisions local to each warehouse. Central allocation would prevent some cross-warehouse overselling, but warehouse operations must continue during a network partition. The choice affects stored inventory assignments and several offline clients, so changing it would require a migration. A short decision record can capture the offline requirement, the choice of local allocation and the accepted reconciliation cost. A later task to unify allocation can refer to the ADR and ask what evidence supports replacing the original trade-off. That last instruction is an adaptation. `SRC-0009:C04` supports recording the rationale. [Two artifacts, two bars](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#two-artifacts-two-bars)

Its two candidate applications are agreeing on domain terms during design and preserving the rationale for a decision that future edits may disturb.

## Related work, conflicts, and limitations

The linked repository artifacts have the same provenance as the practitioner article. They corroborate what the skill tells an agent to do and add examples of file structure and qualifying decisions. The linked issues add scoped user reports. The ADR separation issue’s status has changed since the article’s account. The article’s legacy-file advice concerns Pocock’s skill distribution. It traces the rename from `CONTEXT.md` and `CONTEXT-MAP.md` to the glossary names to the October 4 [distribution release 1.3.0](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/releases/tag/v1.3.0). The setup-assisted migration arrived in a separate October 29 [patch 1.3.2, #1228](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/releases/tag/v1.3.2). It says that `/ubiquitous-language` was removed and the continuing vocabulary work moved into `/domain-modeling`. Repository teams can carry over the practice with their own paths and formats. [Common questions](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#common-questions) · [Issue #557](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/557) · [Issue #717](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/717)

The FAQ acknowledges maintenance burden, stale docs and polished language covering a badly understood model. Its claimed value is strongest upstream, where the team and its reviewers need shared concepts. The article offers no comparison of prompts, records of future changes protected by ADRs, or evidence across representative projects. For an ephemeral project or terminology confined to one conversation, the maintenance cost might outweigh the expected later use. [Common questions, glossary value and vague prompts](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-domain-modeling#common-questions)
