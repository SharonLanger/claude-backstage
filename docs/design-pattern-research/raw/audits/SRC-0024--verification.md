# Independent source review of SRC-0024

**First review — PASS WITH CHANGES.** The paper supports a pooled rank difference in commits and lines changed across selected merged pull requests. The public replication notebooks expose discrepancies in the units counted, the file-breadth comparison, and the labels on the plots. These affect which findings can inform a decision about an unusually large agent PR. [Source dossier](../sources/SRC-0024--sizes-of-agent-pull-requests.md) · [Versioned paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17581v3) · [Replication repository](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026)

## Numbered findings on the submitted dossier

The notebook locations below refer to zero-based cell indexes in the authors' tagged v1.0.0 files. I inspected code, saved tabular outputs and plot labels and compared them to the published v3 PDF. This review did not rerun the notebooks on a frozen GitHub snapshot.

1. **SRC-0024:C01 — correct the unit and selection stage.** The paper gives 932,791 Agentic PRs, 6,618 Human PRs and 116,211 repositories for its source release. The RQ1 load output shows 33,596 Agentic and 6,618 Human PR rows. The merge filter yields 24,014 and 5,081, and the joined per-PR aggregates contain 24,014 and 5,080. These are distinct denominators. The paper calls 440,295 and 23,242 valid-patch commits. In the RQ2 notebooks, those exact figures count rows obtained by joining file-level details to merged PRs. See finding 7. [Paper §2, steps 1–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17581v3#page=2) · [RQ1 cells 2–4 and 8](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ1_patch_characteristics.ipynb)
2. **SRC-0024:C02 — qualify the filter.** The Human REST reconstruction and risk of missing histories or truncated patches are correctly attributed. The publication specifies valid patches for every modified file. The released RQ1 aggregation shows no corresponding per-file completeness check. RQ2 joins the available patch text and removes PRs with empty concatenated diffs. A PR with partial patches can satisfy this latter test if some text remains. The net selection bias remains unknown. [Paper §§2 and 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17581v3) · [Challenge FAQ, Q2](https://conf.researchr.org/track/msr-2026/msr-2026-mining-challenge) · [Agentic RQ2 cells 1–5](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ2_AgenticPRs_code_description_consistency.ipynb) · [Human RQ2 cells 4–10](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ2_HumanPRs_code_description_consistency.ipynb)
3. **SRC-0024:C03 — pass.** Table 2 in the dataset builders' earlier paper documents observable attribution using account, branch and PR-body conventions. That release's human comparison sampled 6,628 PRs from popular repositories, using an explicit exclusion for detected agent PRs. The later study inherits labels. An unmarked use of assistance or an agent acting alongside a human remains possible. The dossier preserves the release difference and the lack of same-task assignment. [AIDev §§3.1–3.2 and Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15003v1#S3) · [Study §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17581v3)
4. **SRC-0024:C04 — correct the breadth metric.** The paper describes distinct paths across all commits and counts renames as one modification. RQ1 cell 4 counts unique filenames on the Agentic side. Cell 8 sums the Human commit-level changed-file counts. A path changed in three Human commits can therefore contribute three to that group's measure. The reported files-touched effect does not support a like-for-like comparison of distinct paths without new analysis. Both sides use unique SHAs for commit counts and sum added and deleted lines. [Paper §2, steps 2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17581v3#page=2) · [RQ1 cells 4 and 8](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ1_patch_characteristics.ipynb)
5. **SRC-0024:C05 — retain the pooled statistics, qualify the inference.** Table 1 and RQ1 cell 13 agree on all five Cliff's deltas and the reported significance cutoff. The notebook passes Human as the first group, so positive delta indicates that ordering favors higher Human observations. The largest effect, 0.5429, concerns unique commits. The files-touched effect of 0.4487 combines the different definitions in finding 4. Neither the rank differences nor the plot establish a frequency of oversized drafts, work on equivalent tasks, or review burden. [Table 1, PDF p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17581v3#page=3) · [RQ1 cell 13](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ1_patch_characteristics.ipynb)
6. **SRC-0024:C06 — qualify named-tool findings.** In RQ1 cells 14 and 16 the plotted data arrays use the default sorted group keys, while the tick labels follow each group's first appearance. The displayed first label is Human, although Claude_Code sorts first. Human sorts fifth. I confirmed the order of the notebook's saved tick labels against the v3 PDF. The tool-specific spread statements appear in the authors' narrative, but these plots cannot corroborate those attributions. The separate pooled test in cell 13 subsets directly by group. [Figures 2–3 and adjoining prose, PDF p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17581v3#page=3) · [RQ1 cells 10, 13, 14 and 16](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ1_patch_characteristics.ipynb)
7. **SRC-0024:C07 — correct the interpretation of the ratios.** Independent division gives 440,295 / 24,014 = 18.33493 and 23,242 / 5,081 = 4.57430. The released RQ2 code prints the respective numerators from the length of the file-detail joins. This occurs before the PR-level empty-diff filters. The RQ1 code counts distinct commit identifiers per PR. The published totals cannot be used to infer the average distinct commit count or a reversal between mean and rank. [Agentic RQ2 cells 1–4](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ2_AgenticPRs_code_description_consistency.ipynb) · [Human RQ2 cells 3–9](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ2_HumanPRs_code_description_consistency.ipynb)
8. **SRC-0024:C08 — pass for Table 2, qualify the method in the surrounding text.** All four Agentic medians exceed the Human medians as printed. The tagged calculations retain 23,976 Agentic and 5,045 Human PRs after removing empty concatenated patch text. They combine available description fields, feed the concatenated patch into scoring, transform the lexical BM25 score with score / (score + 10), and truncate the per-text encoder input. The Human GraphCodeBERT median of 0.8067 is faithful to Table 2. The saved notebook summary gives 0.806617. These metrics measure text agreement and leave reviewer comprehension and correctness open. [Table 2 and §6, PDF p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17581v3#page=4) · [Agentic RQ2 cells 2–8, 10–14 and 18](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ2_AgenticPRs_code_description_consistency.ipynb) · [Human RQ2 cells 6–14 and 23–24](https://github.com/unlv-evol/AgenticPR-MSR-2026/blob/v1.0.0/notebooks/RQ2_HumanPRs_code_description_consistency.ipynb)
9. **SRC-0024:C09 — pass as author advice.** The authors propose triage using commits and files alongside line count. The conclusion names review effort, defect risk and maintainability as future outcomes. No comparison assigns some changes to be sliced and others to be delivered in one PR. AP-07 can cite the qualified pooled results about merged changes. BP-05 can cite the authors' proposal as indirect rationale. [Study §§4 and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17581v3)

## Independent recheck and final disposition

**Final verdict — PASS.** I reread the repaired dossier, including all nine ledger entries, and checked the revised denominators, file and SHA aggregations, plot ordering, and text filters against the tagged notebooks and publication. The challenge organizers also identify 33,596 as the curated Agentic PR count. The dossier now carries the discrepancy in units through its discussion of structural metrics and limits the pooled results to selected merged changes. The named-agent plots carry an explicit attribution caveat. The AP-07 and BP-05 entries distinguish observed cumulative line churn from the unmeasured effects of slicing and review burden. Findings 1, 2 and 4–8 are resolved. Findings 3 and 9 continue to pass. [Challenge dataset description](https://conf.researchr.org/track/msr-2026/msr-2026-mining-challenge) · [Revised dossier](../sources/SRC-0024--sizes-of-agent-pull-requests.md)

The accepted scope is a source audit of the paper and its public release. The original study's file-breadth and per-tool comparisons remain methodologically limited. No full raw-data reproduction was performed.
