# Independent verification of PAT-0004

**PASS after correction and independent recheck.** The worked contract now supports a decision for unseen inputs. The comparisons and reported results hold up. I reopened the conference paper, the publisher’s journal paper and Table 2, and the current product guide. ## Findings

1. **Establish the check (step 1, evidence rows 1 and 5).** The ICLR experiment uses a common executable suite for success and feedback. Anthropic describes runnable checks and review against a specification. Keeping the oracle under the reviewer’s control and correcting an erroneous expectation across all candidates are practical safeguards contributed by the pattern. SRC-0008:C01–C02, C11 and SRC-0002:C07–C08, C10 match those distinctions. [ICLR §§2–3.1, pp. 2–3](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf#page=3) · [Anthropic, executable checks and review](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#give-claude-a-check-to-run)
2. **Set a fair budget (step 2, evidence row 2).** I checked both APPS comparisons and the denominators. Ten initial programs and up to one sibling repair each produced 66.1% versus 63.1% at a ceiling of 20. Two initial programs and ten sibling repairs each produced 61.8% versus 63.4% at a ceiling of 22. The text compares within each ceiling, distinguishes candidate count from actual cost and explains why a single mixed run cannot estimate a competing policy. The two-program incremental allowance is a worked choice. SRC-0008:C02, C05–C07. [ICLR summary, p. 2](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf#page=2) · [Appendix A, pp. 13–17](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf#page=13)
3. **Separate fresh and repair attempts (step 3, evidence rows 3–4).** The ICLR protocol distinguishes independent draws from repairs based on failures. The journal protocol substitutes a problem-statement-only generation at a scheduled attempt. I compared every row of Table 2 with its own baseline. All nine increased at the later threshold. At the earlier threshold, Codestral and Devstral declined. Table 2 provides the counts, despite an inconsistent count in the accompanying narrative. The protocols share HumanEval and have different authors. The ICLR APPS comparison supplies different tasks. Evidence supports keeping fresh samples in consideration. Separate working copies, concurrent execution and the individual developer’s one-attempt decision are catalog adaptations. SRC-0008:C01, C06 and SRC-0003:C02, C06, C08, C11. [Adnan and Kuhn, RQ3 and debugging protocol, pp. 4–5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-27846-5.pdf?code=7b2&error=cookies_not_supported#page=4) · [Publisher Table 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-27846-5/tables/2?code=7b2&error=cookies_not_supported)
4. **Use observed failures to evaluate diagnoses (step 3, evidence row 4).** Table 1 and Appendix D confirm the rates of 52.60% and 33.30% among sampled repaired programs. Sixteen participants saw two model explanations alongside each assigned program. The 40 starting programs came from 20 APPS tasks. The comparison measures repair success conditional on feedback. It leaves human winner selection and time savings open. The pattern labels its recommendation to test a diagnosis as an adaptation. SRC-0008:C01, C09–C10. [ICLR §4.3 and Table 1, p. 8](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf#page=8) · [Appendix D, p. 24](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf#page=24)
5. **Select or stop (step 4, evidence rows 1, 4, 5).** The guide gives a spec-and-edge-case review example. The ICLR authors limit their tasks to self-contained Python with tests. The entry appropriately leaves maintainer approval, test adequacy, ties and disputed expectations to an explicit decision. Neither paper estimates the probability that a human selects the best repository patch. The run cap gives the user an observable stopping point. SRC-0002:C07–C08, C10 and SRC-0008:C02, C11. [Specification review](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#review-against-your-spec) · [ICLR limitations, p. 9](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9ddc141bdbf9d1db510cefff56c586ad-Paper-Conference.pdf#page=9)

## Usability, transfer and rights

6. I executed the pattern’s unmodified Python block. The repair failed only the two-cent, four-recipient case, returning `[2, 0, 0, 0]`. The fresh candidate passed all four cases. I checked the survivor on another 2,020 combinations (0–100 cents, 1–20 recipients). In every case, it returned the required number of shares in descending order, summing to the total and differing by at most one cent. Without this last explicit requirement a reviewer could admit an uneven allocation for an unseen input. The tests exercise this particular illustration. They do not compare generation policies. A change in recipient priority would send the reader back to step 1.

It reproduces no source code, table, figure, transcript or substantial passage. The journal lists CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. The pattern does not rely on permission to redistribute any publication. All seven outgoing local links to the other pattern, dossiers and audits resolved at review. ## Independent review of the four-source revision

**Verdict: PASS, November 21, 2026.** I reopened the King’s College London copy of the ACM version of record and the three previously cited publications. The new evidence strengthens OB-06 and supports the candidate-comparison premise of BP-08. The prior three-source findings and corrections above still apply. [Ouyang et al., ACM version of record](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/365277933/An_Empirical_Study_of_the_Non-Determinism_of_ChatGPT_in_Code_Generation_Version_of_Record.pdf) · [SRC-0019](../sources/SRC-0019--repeatability-of-code-generation.md) · [Independent source review](SRC-0019--verification.md)

10. **Repeated calls and the 39.63% denominator (row 6, SRC-0019:C01–C03).** The baseline made five separate requests per problem with the same prompt and GPT-3.5 Turbo at temperature 1. Table 2 reports a full one-point spread in fractions of tests passed for 39.63% of HumanEval problems. Consequently, at least one candidate failed every test and another passed every test for each such problem. The denominator is tasks. Output agreement is a different measure in the same table. [Study procedure, §2](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/365277933/An_Empirical_Study_of_the_Non-Determinism_of_ChatGPT_in_Code_Generation_Version_of_Record.pdf#page=5) · [Methods, §3.3](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/365277933/An_Empirical_Study_of_the_Non-Determinism_of_ChatGPT_in_Code_Generation_Version_of_Record.pdf#page=8) · [Table 2](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/365277933/An_Empirical_Study_of_the_Non-Determinism_of_ChatGPT_in_Code_Generation_Version_of_Record.pdf#page=11)
11. **Disagreement, agreement and candidate selection (step 4 and row 6, SRC-0019:C02, C05, C08).** Section 7.2 sorts the five candidates by observed test pass rate after execution and reports recurring error categories among all-failing groups. A common exception category need not imply the same underlying defect. The added advice to examine one concrete disagreement and to check a shared answer against an external expectation respects this distinction. A shared error can occur with independent draws on a difficult problem. Neither the number of requests nor an observed shared failure establishes statistical dependence or a common per-attempt success probability. The revised entry makes the appropriately narrower inference about unsupported independence assumptions. [§7.2, ranking on tests](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/365277933/An_Empirical_Study_of_the_Non-Determinism_of_ChatGPT_in_Code_Generation_Version_of_Record.pdf#page=25) · [§7.2 continued, shared errors](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/365277933/An_Empirical_Study_of_the_Non-Determinism_of_ChatGPT_in_Code_Generation_Version_of_Record.pdf#page=26)
12. **Parallel work, transfer and cost (tradeoffs and source perspectives, SRC-0019:C05, C08).** Section 4.3 varies five choices in a single request versus one choice in each of five requests. It measures output similarity and test performance. It supplies no comparison of elapsed delivery time, reviewer effort, human choice with incomplete tests, or returns from running five engineers or agents concurrently. The entry identifies its suggested local time and effort measurements as adaptations. It recognizes the HumanEval and APPS overlap across studies and correctly identifies CodeContests as another dataset in Ouyang et al. The adaptation and numerical summary use original wording and reproduce no source material requiring a redistribution grant.
